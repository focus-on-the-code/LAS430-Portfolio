Unit 3 Portfolio Deliverable

Course artifact — adapted for public review from Canvas export

Start early, plan ahead, and pace yourself...

The Portfolio Deliverable assignments serve three purposes:  

  • To break down the final project into smaller parts 
  • To ensure your familiarity with the e-portfolio tools 
  • To get feedback on different elements of your portfolio early and often, from your peers and prof.  

 

The Unit 3 Portfolio Deliverable will focus on building 30% to 50% of your portfolio. 

Complete the following actions: 

1. Re-read the Resume Guidance resources

2. Create your resume, and add it to your e-Portfolio Home/Landing page

  • You may use any resume template from the examples provided in our resources, or a version you've used in the past.
  • Your resume should be up-to-date, reflecting you current knowledge, skills, and experience. 

3. Update your biography

  • Make improvements based on the feedback from your Unit 1 draft, and your key insights from Unit 3's discussion. 
  • Include any relevant media (images/audio/video) that will make your bio engaging. 

4. Draft your Personal Statement/Narrative - this first draft will be text only

  • Draft the 500-700 word personal statement/narrative in a separate document using Microsoft Word, or a similar text editor.
    • How to access Office365
  • Add it to your e-Portfolio.
  • Include the file as an editable .DOCX attachment in the deliverable post.
  • To get full credit on this post, Prof. T must be able to access and edit this file

Personal Statement/Narrative: This is where you lay out your narrative, your thesis, your through-line. This is like the introduction paragraph in an essay: it should clearly lay out your thoughts on your liberal arts education, the knowledge and skills you’ve acquired, why they’re important, and how they will benefit you in the future. It will also describe how you will use every subsequent page and artifact to detail your position. Included relevant media (e.g.: images, videos) to add interest to the page.  

The personal statement should be 500-700 words. What matters most is that you lay out a clear, cohesive argument.  

The below framing questions may help you build your personal statement. Keep in mind that there are no right or wrong answers. The point is to explain your position, and how you will support it. You can use one or more of these framing questions, you are not required to use any. They are provided here as they’ve helped past students be successful in this assignment. 

  • What is the significance of your degree coming from a liberal arts institution rooted in the mission, vision, and values of the Adrian Dominican Sisters?  
  • How/why will a degree from SHU help you stand apart from those who’ve earned a degree from somewhere else?  
  • What are the intangible qualities that a liberal arts degree offers, and why are they important?  
  • What are the unique transferable skills you gain from a liberal arts education, and why are they important?  
  • How will you use the knowledge, skills, and abilities you’ve acquired in your future? 

 

5. Update your six Classes & Course work pages

At a minimum, all pages should include: 

  • The title of each specific course you will showcase
  • The artifact from each course should be included on the relevant page, accessible by all
  • The 50-200 word or 1-3 minute audio/video description of each artifact should be drafted and visible on the page 

6. Once complete, create a 5-7 minute video walkthrough of your current portfolio. Be sure to include each of the below elements: 

  • Start with your biography: note any changes you've made since Unit 1, and why.
  • Give an overview of your resume:
    • Discuss the structure of your resume, the key knowledge/skills/experience/credentials you chose to include (or ones you chose to exclude), and why. 
    • Reference any specific Resume Guidance resources you used to build and/or improve your resume
  • Give an overview of your Personal Statement/Narrative
    • Briefly outline your thoughts on your liberal arts education, the knowledge and skills you’ve acquired, why they’re important, and how they will benefit you in the future. 
    • If you selected a framing question, which one did you chose, and why? If not: why not? 
    • This first draft is text-only. The requirements for the final give you the option of a text or video based personal statement. After this first draft, will your final version be text, or video? Why? And what would need to be true to make you change your mind, and chose a different format? 
  • Review all six of your Classes & Coursework pages. Discuss why you selected each class and artifact, and how each class is aligned to your personal statement/narrative? 
  • End your video with two specific, open-ended questions for your classmates to address in their replies. These questions should seek substantiative feedback related to specific content and/or design elements of your portfolio.

7. Embed your video,  attach your Personal Statement/Narrative draft as a .DOCX file, add a link to your e-Portfolio to submit your post. Be sure everyone can access the video, document, and portfolio. 

 

Please review the discussion rubric, writing expectations, and GenAI policy before you post. Make sure you adhere to class rules, guidelines, and expectations.
