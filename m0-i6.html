Priority 6: Student-to-Student Guide – How to Succeed in Prof. T’s Class

Course artifact — adapted for public review from Canvas export

This guide compiles advice from former students in other classes. While not specific to LAS-430, the feedback is relevant. It will help you understand my teaching style, navigate my course, manage your time, and get the most out of our work together.  

Previous students have paved the way, making mistakes so you don't have to. Here’s what they wish they knew on Day 1. 

-Prof. T

 

Master the Logistics & Time Management

  • Start Early in the Week: Do not wait until the due date to start your work. Begin your discussion posts and readings early in the week to reduce stress and give yourself time to actually grasp the concepts. 
  • Schedule "Reply Time": Avoid the trap of doing your initial post and forgetting to log back in. Schedule a second, shorter time block 2–3 days later specifically to reply to classmates. 
  • The Final Project Strategy: The final project is worth roughly 50% of your grade. Do not wait until finals week; start outlining this project earlier than you think you should. 

 

Technical Standards & Formatting

  • MLA Format: Switch your brain to MLA format immediately. Even if you are an upperclassman used to other styles, this class requires MLA for citations and essays. 
  • Conquer Technology Early: Learn the class technology and resources immediately. View these requirements as "on-the-job training" that will help you professionally. 
  • Get a Physical Book: Buy a physical book so you can highlight and annotate as you read. 

 

The Feedback Loop & Communication

  • Read Every Comment: You must read the professor's comments on the previous assignment before starting the next one. The video responses and comments are designed to help you improve your grade week over week. 
  • Ask for Help Immediately: Do not wait until the "12th hour" or the end of the semester to ask for help. Asking for help is a strategy, not a weakness. 
  • A "Safe" Environment: The instructor is personable and creates a safe space for you to be honest about your progress. He is approachable and willing to work with you if you put in the effort. 

 

The Winning Mindset

  • Drop the "General Ed" Attitude: If you treat this as a "checklist" class, it becomes a chore. Realize early that this course builds critical thinking and "multidimensional skills" for real life. 
  • Expect Transformation: Be ready for this class to challenge your thought processes and how you view yourself. It is designed to make you a better person, not just a better student. 
  • Resilience is Key: If you get low scores on the first assignments, don’t panic. Adjust your strategy, and reach out to Prof. T as needed. He can help change the direction of your grades. A bad start does not define your final grade. 
  • Value Your Peers: You will learn as much from the diverse stories and backgrounds of your classmates as you will from the textbook. 

 

Highlights: What to Look Forward To

Despite the workload, students consistently rated these specific parts of the course as highlights: 

  • The Creativity Assignment: A break from standard essays where you get to choose your own activity. One student described writing a poem for this unit as a "heart-opening" experience. 
  • Generative AI: This unit is a "game changer." Learning to use AI properly can help you professionally with drafting emails, articulating thoughts, and speeding up research. 
  • The "Dialogue" Assignment: This unit allows you to ask the questions rather than just answering them. 
  • The Readings: Look forward to the essay regarding practicing the Liberal Arts in prison; it is a powerful example of how the Liberal Arts represent freedom.
