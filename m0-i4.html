Priority 4: Final Assignment Requirements – E-Portfolio

Course artifact — adapted for public review from Canvas export

The TLDR: Your final will be a digital portfolio (e-portfolio) showcasing the knowledge and experience gained through your education at Siena Heights University (SHU).  

Detailed instructions and recommendations are below. You are expected to know and apply this guidance. You will not be successful if you stop at this summary.  

  • Deliverable: a digital portfolio (e-portfolio) showcasing the knowledge and experience gained through your education at Siena Heights University (SHU), including coursework from a selection of your classes, and a video walkthrough of your completed portfolio. 
  • Due: must be submitted on-or-before Thursday, February 26, 11:30 PM eastern time. You must submit the final to pass this class. No exceptions, no extensions, no late work will be accepted for credit. 
  • Requirements and grading: The final assignment is worth 210 points – 56% of your final grade. All of the below elements must be addressed by and/or included in your portfolio:  
    • Portfolio Home/Landing page (10 points) 
    • Personal Statement portfolio page (25 points) 
    • SHU & Dominican Mission portfolio page (10 points) 
    • Classes & Coursework pages: portfolio pages reflecting on six (6) different courses, including samples of completed coursework (120 points) 
    • 7-10 minute Video Walkthrough of your portfolio (20 points)  
    • Overall portfolio organization & aesthetics (25 points) 

Introduction: The Final Assignment 

Your final will be a digital portfolio (e-portfolio) showcasing the knowledge and experience gained through your education at Siena Heights University (SHU).  

Your goal is to create a portfolio that explains the value of your liberal arts education: how/why earning a degree in your major/minor is more valuable because it comes from a liberal arts university in general, and SHU in particular.  

Your portfolio will include completed assignments (artifacts) from a selection of your classes, and narrative throughline that speaks to how your studies have prepared you to be successful in future endeavors. You will also record a video walkthrough of your portfolio, highlighting the content on each page, and reflecting on the assignment itself. 

This assignment is worth 210 points, 56% of your final grade. You cannot pass this course if you do not complete the assignment. 

Your e-portfolio must be submitted on-or-before Thursday, February 26, 11:30 PM eastern time. No exceptions, no extensions, no late work will be accepted for credit. 

I recommend using the Canvas E-Portfolio tools to create your digital portfolio, and the Canvas Studio for all audio/video recordings. This software is provided to you at no cost, and technical support – live and asynchronous – is available through the Canvas platform. The Canvas E-Portfolio and Studio can be used to meet all requirements of the final assignment. You'll find links to tutorial videos in the Learning Resources.

You are welcome to use any tool, software, or platform you wish for portfolio design and development, and audio/video recording and production. The e-portfolio you submit must meet the requirements of the assignment. The technical limitations of the tools you chose will not be considered when grading the final product. Technical support for tools not owned, licensed, hosted, or administered by SHU will be your responsibility, and no SHU faculty, staff, or classmates can be relied upon to fix issues you experience.   

Ultimately: you are responsible for gaining the technical expertise and experience required to complete and submit the assignment, regardless of the tools/software/platform you chose.  

This is a 400-level course. Your portfolio, video, and overall presentation of your work should represent the effort and preparation commensurate with 400-level coursework.  

Start early, plan ahead, and pace yourself. This assignment is your opportunity to showcase your hard work throughout your college career.  

You are ready. You can do this. Put in the work, and you will succeed. 

-Prof. T 

Why This Matters: The New World of Work 

With 18 years' experience in Human Resources (HR) in multi-national fortune 100 companies, I am qualified to say: “work” is changing at an unprecedented rate. How we work, and the expectations of employers and employees are different today than when I started my career. And technological changes over the last decade, if not the last three years, are accelerating the pace of change. The most experienced thought leaders cannot predict what’s next.  

But there are two key things we can count on: 

  1. Deep Reading, Critical Thinking, and Clear Writing are meta-skills that add value to all aspects of work and life.  
  2. Employers want to see your experience, and you must be able to market it well.  

These concepts are not new. Every liberal arts assignment is designed to teach students to read deeply, think critically, and write clearly. Internships and apprenticeships, job training programs, college accreditation and ranking systems exist to qualify and communicate skill acquisition and mastery. A reputable degree or certificate, a resume, and a letter of recommendation communicate knowledge, experience, and capability. However, I believe that increasing focus will be placed on assessing an individual's actual body of work, and how their knowledge, skills, and training influenced the development and delivery of that work. Live coding assessments, work samples and simulations, probationary periods; these are some examples of this concept in practice. 

My success is a product of my education: a liberal arts undergraduate degree, a master's in human resources, and multiple professional trainings and certifications. One key thing that I see in the business and thought leaders I follow: they craft a strong portfolio that showcases their best work. The knowledge, skills, and training that influenced their work, what they gained from their experiences, and how it prepares them to be successful in the future. 

This is your opportunity to practice a skill that will differentiate you from your peers. Once you’ve completed this project, you’ll have a basic framework and experience to build and improve your portfolio on any platform you chose – social media apps, a personal blog, a professional website. The most important thing to take away from this project is the experience of curating your content, and crafting a cohesive narrative that showcases your knowledge, skills, and experience.  

 

E-Portfolio Requirements: 5 Key Elements 

Your e-portfolio will be a multi-media collection of text, images, audio, and video. It will include work completed in this course, and prior work from your major, minor, and liberal arts classes. You will also craft a narrative throughline that explains the value of your liberal arts education: how/why earning a degree in your major(s)/minor(s) is more valuable because it comes from a liberal arts university in general, and SHU in particular. Like the thesis statement in an essay, you will use your selected works to illustrate how your major, minor, and liberal arts studies connect and support the value gained from your education.  

Specifically, your portfolio must include the following five (5) elements and content: 

1. Home page/Landing page: This page must include biographic information about you, and a professional resume in a downloadable format. Included relevant media (e.g.: images, videos) to add interest to the page.  

 

2. Personal Statement/Narrative: This is where you lay out your narrative, your thesis, your throughline. This is like the introduction paragraph in an essay: it should clearly lay out your thoughts on your liberal arts education, the knowledge and skills you’ve acquired, why they’re important, and how they will benefit you in the future. It will also describe how you will use every subsequent page and artifact to detail your position. Included relevant media (e.g.: images, videos) to add interest to the page.  

The personal statement should be 500-700 words, or a 10-12 minute presentation. What matters most is that you lay out a clear, cohesive argument.  

The below framing questions may help you build your personal statement. Keep in mind that there are no right or wrong answers. The point is to explain your position, and how you will support it. You can use one or more of these framing questions, you are not required to use any. They are provided here as they’ve helped past students be successful in this assignment. 

  • What is the significance of your degree coming from a liberal arts institution rooted in the mission, vision, and values of the Adrian Dominican Sisters?  
  • How/why will a degree from SHU help you stand apart from those who’ve earned a degree from somewhere else?  
  • What are the intangible qualities that a liberal arts degree offers, and why are they important?  
  • What are the unique transferable skills you gain from a liberal arts education, and why are they important?  
  • How will you use the knowledge, skills, and abilities you’ve acquired in your future? 

 

3. SHU & Dominican Mission: this page should speak directly to the mission, vision, and values of Siena Heights and the Adrian Dominican Sisters (ADS).  

For example: you could write 300 words on the SHU and ADS mission/vision/values/pillars, and how/why they’ve impacted your education (aligning it to your narrative). Add pictures or videos of you actively living the mission, along with a text, audio, or video narration that explains the importance of the artifacts. 

 

4. Classes & Coursework: You must include reflections and artifacts from six (6) different courses in your university education:  

  • Two (2) must be LAS courses taken at SHU (e.g.: LAS-330, LAS-430). 
  • Two (2) must be courses in your major/minor taken at SHU. 
  • Two (2) must be courses outside of your major/minor, taken at SHU or transfer courses that best represent the ADS values/pillars, and/or the liberal arts. 

The page for every course in your e-portfolio must have a 300-400 word written reflection that includes: 

  • A brief description of the course in your own words 
  • Your views/feelings about the course prior to engaging in the coursework 
  • An evaluation of how you demonstrated the outcomes of the course  
  • Your views/feelings about the course after you finished 
  • An analysis of how the course adds value to your work and/or life after you graduate (aligning it to your narrative) 

Every course page must also include at least one artifact from the specific course. Artifacts can be substantial papers, presentations, creative work, or a mix of any of this content produced in and for the specific class. You can have more than one artifact for each class. Each artifact must be accompanied by a brief 50-200 word or 1-3 minute audio/video explanation of the artifact, why it was selected, and its importance (aligning it to your narrative). 

 

5. Video Walkthrough: Once the portfolio is complete, create a 7-10 minute video tour of your e-portfolio, and embed the video on your home page. The video should include a brief personal introduction, cover every page of your portfolio, and highlight all content and artifacts on each page. Throughout the video, integrate key points from your personal statement/narrative explaining the value of your liberal arts education, and how each course contributed to the knowledge, skills, and experiences you’ve gained.  

The video walkthrough is not an opportunity to read pages from your portfolio verbatim. You will lose points for doing so. You should prepare and practice specific details and key points you will speak to in your video walkthrough to ensure you deliver a polished, engaging, thoughtful overview of your portfolio, and a clear, concise summary of your narrative – how/why you value your liberal arts education. 

 

Submitting the Portfolio: Due Thursday, February 26, 11:30pm eastern. No exception.  

You will embed the video walkthrough, and post a link to your e-portfolio, in the Final Assignment discussion.  

All work must be submitted on-or-before Thursday, February 26, 11:30pm eastern time. No exceptions, no extensions, no late work will be accepted for credit. 

  • The walkthrough video should be embedded in the discussion post, so that is it playable within the discussion itself.  
  • The link to your e-portfolio must be clear and easy to find. Your portfolio must be accessible by all classmates, and your professor, without having to request and approve access.  
  • Failure to meet these submission requirements will result in points deducted from the assignment. 

 

Grading Rubric:  

The final assignment is worth 210 points – 56% of your final grade. You cannot pass this class without submitting a final assignment.  

The final assignment must be submitted on-or-before Thursday, February 26, 11:30pm eastern time. No exceptions, no extensions, no late work will be accepted for credit. 

Your portfolio will be assessed on how well it meets the requirements set forth in this document. Each of the five key elements will be scored, as well as the overall organization and aesthetics of your e-portfolio. All content – audio/video/text – will be assessed based on your use of college level grammar, writing conventions, presentation delivery, and MLA citation accuracy (where applicable). Prior assignments submitted as artifacts will not be graded, and their grades will not impact the grade of this final assignment.  

Said differently:  

  • Your portfolio should be well organized and easy to navigate. 
  • All content – audio/video/text – should be clear, compelling, well written and/or presented. 
  • All content should be accessible by the end user: if there’s a video on your page, I should be able to play it; if you provide a link to your resume, I should be able to access and download a copy.  
  • All images and videos should be clear and easy to view: appropriate size for pictures, embedded videos where possible. 
  • All videos should accommodate accessibility concerns: closed captions should be on, and captions should be edited for accuracy. 
  • Ideas summarized or paraphrased, and quotations from work that is not you own should be correctly cited in MLA format. This includes in-text citations, and a Work Cited list (as footnotes at the bottom of the page, or a separate page collecting all citations). 

 

Scoring Breakdown: 210 total points, by portfolio element 

  • Home/Landing Page (10 points) 
  • Personal Statement (25) 
  • SHU & Dominican Mission (10) 
  • Classes & Coursework (120) 
  • Video Walkthrough (20)  
  • Portfolio Organization & Aesthetics (25) 

 

Extra Credit: Final Assignment Replies – Due Friday, February 27, 11:30pm eastern.  

You can earn 10 extra credit points on the final assignment by posting a reply to up to five of your classmates' videos (2 points per reply). Your replies must be 100-words minimum, illustrate that you’ve both watched the video walkthrough and explored their e-portfolio, and follow expectations set for other portfolio deliverable replies.  

Replies must be submitted on-or-before Friday, February 27, 11:30pm eastern time. No late replies will be accepted for extra credit.
