Unit 6 Overview

Course artifact — adapted for public review from Canvas export

In this unit students will explore the importance of argument and debate in democratic society

Assignments

Reading +

  • Chapters 4 & 7 in Not for Profit
  • Paul V. Griffith, Arguing For Democracy: and interview with Vanderbilt philosophers Scott F. Aikin and Robert B. Talisse
  • The Socratic Method - a primer by Gail Ryder
  • Disputatio and the Dominican Tradition - lecture by G. Ryder (Video and Transcript)

  • Debate Like a World Champion: A Conversation with Bo Seo, Harvard Debate Coach - Open to Debate (Youtube) 

  • Is Social Media Good for Democracy? - a debate - Open to Debate (YouTube)

 

  • Prof. T - Critical Thinking

  • Prof. T - Critical Thinking with AI

 

Discussion & Replies

  • Address all questions in the Discussion assignment
  • Reply to three classmates' posts

 

Final Reflection

  • Take a moment to reflect on the material we've covered, and the class discussion posts.

 

This Unit Meets the following course outcomes:

  • Students will synthesize ideas, content, and methods of various disciplines 
  • Students will demonstrate information literacy and technical proficiency appropriate for scholarly and professional work 
  • Students will analyze course material (written, visual, oral, presentation, etc.) to elevate critical thinking skills 
  • Students will explore civic and social responsibility 
  • Students will demonstrate a sense of social and civic responsibility that supports (aligns with) the Dominican tradition
