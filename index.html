Teaching design · challenge and response

Making a seven-week, asynchronous capstone achievable

Students were asked to synthesize learning from across their liberal arts education and communicate that journey through a public-facing e-portfolio. Working from an existing Canvas shell, I organized priorities, readings, assignment instructions, and milestones so students could work independently while receiving feedback before the final submission.

The course was structured around two connected learning strands: humanities inquiry through readings, discussion, and reflection; and portfolio construction through research, successive drafts, peer response, and instructor guidance. The goal was a coherent academic experience—not a collection of unrelated weekly tasks.

Portfolio milestones · research, application, feedback

A cumulative assignment built through deliberate practice

Unit 1 — Explore and launch

Read / research: Learn the course workflow, Canvas portfolio tools, and expectations.

Portfolio deliverable: Create the initial e-portfolio and share an early walkthrough for feedback.

Unit 3 — Research and build · 30–50%

Read / research: Study student, creative, and professional portfolio formats and current résumé resources; evaluate what makes examples effective.

Portfolio deliverable: Apply findings to portfolio choices; add a résumé, revised biography, and 500–700-word personal narrative.

Read / research: Explore creativity and imagination through assigned reading and related resources; revisit prior portfolio examples and feedback.

Portfolio deliverable: Revise the résumé, biography, narrative, and portfolio presentation; continue the video walkthrough.

Online pedagogy · clarity and feedback

Designed for students working on their own schedules

Find the answer before asking

The priority-ordered orientation collects weekly requirements, writing standards, the final-project brief, portfolio support, tutorials, and the course’s generative AI expectations.

Know what happens next

Unit overview pages and repeating discussion/assignment patterns give students an understandable pathway through a compressed term.

Practice, receive feedback, then revise

Draft portfolio checkpoints, video walkthroughs, peer replies, and instructor comments allow students to improve work ahead of the final portfolio.

Instructional decisions

Student-facing clarity

Course policies and task requirements were collected into a priority-based orientation so online students could find expectations and resources independently.

Feedback before final grading

Intermediate deliverables made room for portfolio walkthroughs, instructor guidance, classmate responses, and iteration.

Academic and practical synthesis

Students combined research and critical reflection with a digital portfolio, written narrative, and multimedia presentation.
