Priority 5: Productivity

Course artifact — adapted for public review from Canvas export

The TLDR: Productivity = Getting things done in time 

  • Productivity here means getting important things done in the time you actually have. 
  • Three tools do most of the work: a calendar, a task list, and the “What’s the next action?” mindset. 
  • The system reduces stress by moving plans out of your head and into tools you can trust. 

Continue reading the full document below. You will not be successful if you stop at this summary.   

 

 

Philosophy & Context 

Your time is finite. Spend it deliberately. 

Productivity is getting things done in time, not just on time. Everyone has the same 24 hours, but your usable time shrinks once you factor in sleep, meals, work, athletics, and other classes. The goal is to focus on what matters and finish it within the time window you actually have. 

 

The Three Tools 

Simple tools. Real results. 

The system is intentionally basic: one calendar to anchor time, one task list to capture actions, and one question to turn big assignments into doable steps. 

 

1) Calendar 

If it’s not in the calendar, it doesn’t exist. 

What the calendar is for 

  • Anything that must happen at a specific day or time. 
  • Time blocks for work sessions when you know you will need focused time. 
  • Automatic reminders so you do not rely on memory. 

How to use it well 

  • Put everything important in one digital calendar and turn on notifications. 
  • Block time to work on assignments before they are due. 
  • Use Canvas, Apple Calendar, Google Calendar, or Outlook, but pick one primary home. 

Examples from the video 

  • Assignments due on set days show up clearly in the Canvas calendar. 
  • If an action has a due date, put that due date in the calendar. 
  • If you need a work session, schedule the work session, not just the deadline. 

 

2) Task List 

Your brain is for thinking, not tracking. 

What the task list is for 

  • A single place to capture what you need to do. 
  • Clarity on what to do, by when, and by who (owner). 
  • A way to get tasks out of your head so you can focus. 

How to use it well 

  • Do a brain dump: get all tasks and ideas out of your head and into the list. 
  • Review your list daily for urgent items and weekly to plan what is next. 
  • Use a notebook, Microsoft To Do, Apple Reminders, or Google Tasks, but keep one trusted list. 

Why this matters 

When tasks live only in your head, they create mental clutter. A task list frees attention for learning, planning, and doing. 

 

3) The Next Action Mindset 

Big tasks move only through small steps. 

What “next action” means 

  • Not the whole project. 
  • Not the final deliverable. 
  • The very next small, simple step you can actually do. 

Examples 

  • Oil change: find a shop, call, schedule, drive there. 
  • Discussion post: read the prompt, schedule drafting time, schedule editing time, post it. 

How it reduces procrastination 

A clear next action lowers friction. Once the first small step is defined, momentum is easier to build. 

 

How the Tools Fit Together 

Capture. Clarify. Schedule. 

 

Recommended workflow 

  1. Capture the task in your task list (what to do, by when, by who). 
  2. Clarify the next action for that task (the smallest doable step). 
  3. Decide: if it takes two minutes or less, do it now. If not, schedule time for it. 
  4. Schedule due dates and work sessions in your calendar so you can see the week clearly. 
  5. Review daily and weekly so nothing slips.

 

Quick Start: Do One Thing Today 

Help future you. Start small. 

  • Pick one calendar app and learn the basics (events, reminders, notifications). 
  • Do a brain dump into a task list or notebook to clear mental space. 
  • Schedule one important thing you have been putting off. 
  • Take one task and write the next action in plain language. 

Do not try to overhaul everything at once. Pick one move today and build from there. 

 

Risks & Considerations 

Tools help, but they do not think for you. 

  • Over-scheduling can backfire. Leave buffer time for travel, delays, and rest. 
  • A task list can become noise if you never review it. Daily and weekly review is the difference. 
  • Notifications are helpful until they are constant. Tune reminders so you notice the important ones. 

 

Tool Selection 

Use what you already have. 

  • Calendar: Apple Calendar, Google Calendar, Outlook, or the Canvas calendar. 
  • Task list: Microsoft To Do, Apple Reminders, Google Tasks, or a simple notebook. 
  • If tech access is limited, a paper planner plus a pocket notebook works.
