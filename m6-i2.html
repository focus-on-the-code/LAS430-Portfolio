GenAI Experiment & 5+ minute Reflection – to complete this assignment, you will use a Generative AI chat bot for critical thinking

This assignment will help you learn how LLMs work, and one way to use them as a thought partner. 

Follow each of these steps: 

Before using AI

    1. Watch Prof. T's two videos: Critical Thinking, and GenAI Assignment & Demo (below, and in the Unit 6 Overview). 
    2. Watch the debate video, Is Social Media Good for Democracy, or read the AI Generated Summary.
    3. Determine your position: do you think Social Media is good or bad for democracy? 
    4. Review Prof. T's sample conversations: this will give you an idea of what to expect.
      • Prof. T & ChatGPT
      • ChatGPT & Chat GPT
      • Prof. T & Gemini
      • Gemini & Gemini

AI Experiment

Choose a chat bot: ChatGPT,  Google Gemini, Claude.ai, Perplexity.ai, Microsoft CoPilot (available with your Siena Heights Office 365 subscription). Any of the free chat bots will work.

Experiment 1: You + AI

  1. Download this PDF doc (You+AI Prompt), and copy-paste the full prompt into the AI chat window
  2. Answer all questions from the bot: the more context you provide, the more interesting the conversation.
  3. The chatbot will confirm when you're done. Copy-paste the whole conversation into a separate document, and add it to your discussion post.

Experiment 2: AI + AI

  1. Download this PDF doc (AI+AI Prompt), and copy-paste the full prompt into the AI chat window
  2. Read along as the bot asks and answers questions, presenting both sides of the argument.
  3. The chatbot will confirm when its done. Copy-paste the whole conversation into a separate document, and add it to your discussion post.

 

5+ minute recorded Reflection 

In a 5+ minute video recording, show your conversation with the AI chatbot, and reflect on your experience

There's no slides or essay required. Spend five minutes addressing any five (5) questions below, with your chatbot conversation on the screen.

  • Challenge: Show us what part of either conversation made you uncomfortable, defensive, or stuck. What do you think that reveals about your thinking?
  • Delight/curiosity: What part of either conversation delighted you, intrigued you, or made you want to keep going?
  • Assumption surfaced: Identify one hidden assumption you noticed in yourself. Show us the exact line/turn where it occurs?
  • Assumption revised: Did you or the chatbot revise or soften any assumption? If yes, show the exact line/turn where that shift happened.
  • Bot behavior: Did the bot stay in “question-asking mode,” or did it start lecturing? Point to one place it did it well, and one place it didn’t.
  • Help moments: When you said, “I don’t know,” or asked for help, did the bot’s guidance help you move forward, or take over your thinking?
  • Your agency: Show us where you felt most “in charge” of the thinking? Is there a place where you felt the bot was steering too much?
  • What would you do differently: If you repeated either experiment, what would you change in either prompt or approach for a better conversation?
  • Skill transfer: Where else (another class, job decision, civic issue, personal dilemma) could you use this “AI as Socratic partner” method, and how?

 

To Submit your Reflection post

  1. Embed your 5+ minute recorded reflection in the assignment text box
  2. Attach the transcripts of your GenAI experiments as two (2) separate documents: 
    • You + AI Conversation
    • AI + AI Conversation

 

EXTRA CREDIT Opportunity!!

You can earn one (1) extra credit point for replying to your classmate's posts (up to ten (10) extra credit points max)

Replies will follow similar grading standards for discussion replies: 

  • 200+ words.
  • Must specifically address key points in your classmates’ discussion post: providing related insights and analysis, addressing/raising questions, commentary that adds to the conversation. 
  • Go beyond “I agree.” Add insight, ask a follow-up question, connect their ideas to other material.   
  • Replies may be any combination of the following:  
    • Replies to different classmates' discussion posts. 
    • Replies to a classmates' reply on your post. 
    • Replies to a classmates' reply on any post. 
  • NOT Acceptable: A reply to your own post or reply.  
  • Start by replying to classmates who’s posts have few or no replies to ensure we are all included in the conversation. We’re all in this together, so let’s support everyone as best as we can!

 

Please review the discussion rubric, writing expectations, and GenAI policy before you post. Make sure you adhere to class rules, guidelines, and writing expectations.
